Tyrannosaurus rex: A Field Profile

Tyrannosaurus rex was a large theropod dinosaur that lived during the late
Cretaceous period, roughly 68 to 66 million years ago. It was one of the last
non-avian dinosaurs to exist before the Cretaceous-Paleogene extinction event.

Anatomy and size. Adult T. rex individuals could reach lengths of around 12
metres and weigh between 8 and 14 metric tonnes. Its skull alone could exceed
1.5 metres in length, and its bite force is among the highest estimated for any
land animal that has ever lived.

Diet and behaviour. T. rex was an apex predator and likely both hunted live
prey and scavenged carcasses. Debate continues among palaeontologists about the
balance between active predation and opportunistic scavenging in its diet.

Fossil record. Dozens of T. rex skeletons have been recovered, primarily from
the Hell Creek Formation in North America. These specimens have made T. rex one
of the best-studied of all dinosaurs and a fixture of natural history museums
around the world.
